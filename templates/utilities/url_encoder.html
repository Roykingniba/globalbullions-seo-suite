Why URLs need encoding

URLs can only contain a limited set of characters. Anything else, including spaces, accented letters and most punctuation, has to be written as a percent sign followed by a code. A space becomes %20, and an ampersand becomes %26.

The ampersand matters most. Inside a URL, & separates one parameter from the next. If a value you are inserting contains an ampersand and you do not encode it, the URL breaks in a way that is easy to miss: everything after it gets read as a separate parameter.
