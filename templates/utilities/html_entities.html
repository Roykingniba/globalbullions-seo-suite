When you need this

Some characters mean something specific to a browser. A < starts a tag, and an & starts an entity. If you want those characters to appear on the page as themselves rather than being interpreted, they have to be written as entities: &lt; and &amp;.

This comes up most often when you are writing about code. To show a reader an example tag, you have to encode it, or the browser will render the tag instead of displaying it.

Encoding quote marks matters when you are placing a value inside an HTML attribute. An unencoded quote closes the attribute early and breaks the tag.
